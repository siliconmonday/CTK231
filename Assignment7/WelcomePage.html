This is a link. Theatrical pop-rocker Silicon Monday has released his first full-length album, a melodramatic jaunt through heartbreak and healing. Inspired in no small part by the lyricism of the bygone age of emo, Monday weaves together rich harmonies and quirky instrumentals with defiant Alice Cooper-esque rasp. In just under twenty-five minutes, the record—described as “twee and Broadway-ish” by The Joy of Violent Movement—twists and turns through accordion-filled baroque pop (“Seasoned Assist” , “A Girl Named Mercy (The Mariposa Song)”), synthy glam- rock (“The Ballad of Lorna Lincoln”), a saccharine piano ballad (“Zeroed Out (Beautiful Again)”), steel drum-infused tropical novelty (“Muscle Relaxer”), and quippy industrial grit (“Wings of Glass”). They shouldn’t work together—but somehow, tied together by the overarching theme of metamorphosis through reflection and sheer force of will, they do. Monday cements himself as a versatile and sophisticated musician; we can only hope his sophomore record will be more batshit insane. METAM by Silicon Monday is available on all streaming platforms.
